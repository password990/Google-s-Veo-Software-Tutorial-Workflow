# Sample narration: Build and check a decision brief from example files

**Approximate duration:** 1:50 spoken content plus short visual beats in a 2:00 cut. The example pack is invented training material prepared before recording.

## 0:00 — Result preview (voiceover over the actual final document)

This decision brief pulls together meeting minutes, an informal survey, and a planning budget. I’ll show how the files fit together, use them in a research assistant, and check its key figures against the originals.

## 0:10 — Presenter

I prepared these sample documents first so the example has realistic source material. They describe a invented community garden proposal. The figures and people are invented, and the files are labeled as training examples.

## 0:24 — Source document capture

Here are the three source files: the planning minutes, the neighbourhood pulse survey, and the preliminary budget note. Notice that the survey is self-selected and the cost figures are estimates, not supplier quotes. Those limits need to stay in the final brief.

## 0:40 — Presenter

The important part is keeping the facts consistent. We’ll ask the assistant to summarize only these files, and we’ll check the result ourselves before using it.

## 0:52 — App capture

I’m adding the three source documents to the selected research tool. I’ll confirm each filename is present before I ask the question.

## 1:03 — App capture

Now I’ll request a short decision brief. It should explain the recommendation, list the conditions that still need to be met, and cite the sample files by name and section. It must not turn a proposal into an approval.

## 1:22 — App capture

I’ll let the response finish before reviewing it. A loading screen is not a result, and a polished answer still needs checking.

## 1:29 — Verify against the source files

The brief says 59 of 86 respondents supported exploring a pilot. That is 68.6 percent when rounded to one decimal place. The budget note lists 6,850 dollars for setup, but it calls that a preliminary estimate. I’m checking both details in the original files now.

## 1:45 — Presenter

That’s the workflow: build a believable source pack, use those exact files in the app, then verify important claims against the originals. The documents make the demonstration concrete; checking them keeps it honest.

## 1:56 — End card

Prepare the sources. Build the brief. Check the evidence.
