RECOMMENDATION Authorize a short feasibility and quote-gathering phase for a four-bed pilot. Do not authorize construction or purchases yet. Return for a funding decision after the site, accessible route, volunteer rota, and supplier prices are confirmed.

Purpose

This brief combines the working group minutes, informal neighbourhood survey, and preliminary cost note to support a decision on the next planning step. It does not establish site permission, final design, or a confirmed budget.

Why consider a pilot

The pulse survey found early interest: 59 of 86 respondents (68.6%) supported exploring a small garden. Respondents most often selected access to fresh produce (62 selections), opportunities to meet neighbours (55), and hands-on learning for young people (44). Because responses were self-selected, these results should guide follow-up questions rather than be presented as a neighbourhood-wide mandate. [Source: 02_Neighbourhood_Pulse_Survey_Summary, “Headline result” and “What respondents mentioned.”]

Planning cost range

The preliminary estimate is $6,850 for one-time setup and $1,750 per year for operations. A 10% setup reserve would produce a provisional ceiling of $7,535 before tax. The estimate excludes tax, permitting, major drainage work, insurance changes, and volunteer labour; it is an illustrative USD planning estimate, not a quote. [Source: 03_Pilot_Budget_and_Operating_Note, “One-time setup estimate,” “Annual operating estimate,” and “Assumptions and exclusions.”]

Decision requested

  • Approve a feasibility phase only, with no purchases or construction commitment.
  • Ask the named owners to close the four open conditions and return with evidence.
  • Invite additional input, including from residents who were unsure or opposed in the pulse survey.

Proposed review point

At the next working-group meeting, review the written site response, accessible layout, volunteer rota, and comparable supplier quotes. If any condition remains unresolved, revise the scope or defer the funding request.
